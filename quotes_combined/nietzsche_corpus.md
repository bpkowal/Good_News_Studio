## Will to Power as Moral Compass

Nietzsche rejects conventional morality rooted in weakness, instead proposing that moral value lies in strength, vitality, and the will to overcome.

> "What is good?—All that heightens the feeling of power, the will to power, power itself in man.  
> What is bad?—All that proceeds from weakness."

## A Good War Justifies Any Cause

> “You say 'a good cause justifies any war'? I say: a good war justifies any cause.”

This quote serves as a direct challenge to moral theories that seek to justify actions through abstract causes, such as utility or fairness. Nietzsche provocatively inverts this logic, implying that strength, vitality, and struggle—not abstract morality—give life its meaning and generate value. This quote aligns with Nietzsche's master morality, where value is created, not discovered.

## Herd Morality and the Denial of Life

Nietzsche critiques what he calls “herd morality”—a system that rewards obedience, passivity, and equality by suppressing strength, ambition, and vital individuality. He warns that such morality is not neutral but life-denying.

> "Morality in Europe today is herd animal morality—in other words, as we understand it, merely one type of human morality beside which, before which, and after which many other moralities, and above all higher moralities, are or should be possible."

## The Lie as a Condition of Life

Nietzsche provocatively questions the pursuit of truth as a foundational value, suggesting instead that illusion, deception, and self-constructed meaning are necessary for life and vitality.

> “The falseness of a judgment is not necessarily an objection to a judgment... The question is to what extent it is life-promoting, life-preserving, species-preserving, perhaps even species-cultivating.”


---

## The Metaphysical Faith Behind Science

Nietzsche challenges the assumption that science is value-free, revealing that even secular truth-seeking rests on ancient metaphysical and theological beliefs.

> "It is still a metaphysical faith upon which our faith in science rests—that even we seekers after knowledge today, we godless anti-metaphysicians still take our fire from the flame lit by a faith that is thousands of years old, that Christian faith, which was also the faith of Plato, that God is truth; that truth is divine. But what if this should become more and more incredible, if nothing should prove divine any longer unless it be error, blindness, lies—if God himself should prove to be our most enduring lie?"

Nietzsche insists that morality should not be approached as an impersonal set of abstract rules. True engagement with ethical life requires a personal stake—it demands passion, ownership, and authenticity.

> “'Selflessness' has no value either in heaven or on earth; the great problems demand great love, and only strong, round, secure spirits who have a firm grip on themselves are capable of it.”  
> (The Gay Science, Book V §345)
