Ethical judgment

Recommendation: Pull the lever, redirecting the trolley toward one person
Preference strength: 0.80
Epistemic confidence: 0.70 (likelihood the judgment survives further factual inquiry and scrutiny)

Central reason: Given the explicit certainties, the moral weight of preventing four additional deaths overrides the moral cost of becoming the direct cause of one death. While deontological concerns about direct harm carry significant force, they are not sufficient here to outweigh the clear, magnitude-based benefit of saving four extra lives under conditions of certainty. Thus, pulling the lever is ethically preferable.

Ethical alternatives:
- ACTION 1 (Pull): Directly saves four net lives; aligns with utilitarian minimization of harm; respects widely‐held intuitions about preventing a larger loss of life when the only alternative is a smaller one; action/inaction asymmetry is outweighed by the magnitude gap (five vs. one).
- ACTION 2 (Do not pull): Avoids actively killing; preserves deontological prohibition on intentional harm; maintains moral innocence regarding the one person’s death; acknowledges moral uncertainty about measuring lives purely by quantity and about the legitimacy of overriding rights by utilitarian calculus.

Catastrophic-risk check: No explicitly stated low-probability catastrophic risks beyond the certain deaths described. No hidden downside is mentioned, so catastrophic risk differential is effectively zero between options.

Reconsider if: Discovery that pulling the lever would in fact kill more than one person or spare fewer than five.; Evidence that the one person has an overriding moral claim (e.g., is the only doctor who can save the five later) sufficient to reverse the utility balance.; Adoption of a strict deontological rule that intentional killing is impermissible regardless of consequences..
