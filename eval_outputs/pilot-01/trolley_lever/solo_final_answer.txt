Ethical judgment

Recommendation: Pull the lever, redirecting the trolley toward one person
Support: 0.77

Central reason: Given equal moral status among the potential victims, five deaths constitute a substantially greater moral harm than one. While pulling the lever makes you causally responsible for a death, failing to act makes you predictably responsible for five. Under most ethical frameworks that balance consequences and duties, the obligation to minimize preventable loss of life decisively favors redirecting the trolley. Residual deontological concern about commission vs. omission is acknowledged but judged insufficient to override the 5:1 life-saving differential. Therefore, the ethically recommended action is to pull the lever.

Ethical alternatives:
- ACTION 1 – Pull the lever: Minimizes total deaths (1 vs. 5), thus better satisfies widely-held moral principles that give strong weight to each person’s interest in continued life; aligns with utilitarian calculus, many forms of contractualism, and commonsense beneficence. Responsibility for the redirected death is outweighed by duty to prevent the greater harm that you foresee and can avert.
- ACTION 2 – Do not pull the lever: Avoids directly causing a death through your own intervention, respecting deontological constraints against intentionally killing an innocent; preserves moral innocence (omission vs. commission) and respects the one person’s right not to be used as a means to save others, reflecting strong non-consequentialist intuitions.

Catastrophic-risk check: No explicit low-probability catastrophic outcomes beyond the specified fatalities. Pulling the lever introduces no further systemic or long-tail harms; not pulling leaves the higher fatality count. Thus expected catastrophic risk is strictly higher inaction.

Reconsider if: Discovery that the one person is uniquely critical to preventing an even greater future catastrophe.; Evidence that pulling the lever would trigger secondary events causing more than five deaths.; A validated moral theory assigning vastly greater weight to the wrongness of direct killing over preventing deaths..
