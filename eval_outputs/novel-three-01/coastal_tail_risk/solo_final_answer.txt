Ethical judgment

Recommendation: Reject the project
Preference strength: 0.70
Epistemic confidence: 0.60 (likelihood the judgment survives further factual inquiry and scrutiny)

Central reason: Weighing expected values, 600,000 job-years benefit a current population, while even the expected displacement figure (60,000 people) already matches that scale, and the full-magnitude outcome is an order of magnitude worse. Irreversibility and moral asymmetry between causing future catastrophe versus allowing current economic hardship give greater ethical weight to avoiding collapse. Present hardship from job loss, though severe, is non-catastrophic and potentially addressable by policy, whereas ecological collapse is not. Therefore, rejecting the project is ethically preferable, with moderate decisiveness and moderate confidence given uncertainties about future mitigation options.

Ethical alternatives:
- Approve: Guarantees 30,000 current workers and their dependents (≈100,000 people) a stable income for two decades, preventing immediate hardship, regional economic decline, and follow-on social ills. The 3 % collapse risk may never materialize; technological or policy advances in the next 50 years could mitigate damage even if an ecological downturn begins. Discounting future outcomes, present welfare gains may ethically outweigh uncertain, future harms.
- Reject: Eliminates a low-probability (3 %) but catastrophic, irreversible ecological event whose expected dislocation (0.03 × 2,000,000 = 60,000 people) already rivals the total workers helped, and whose worst-case magnitude (2 million forced migrants, ecosystem loss) vastly exceeds the job benefit. The moral duty to avoid potentially irreparable harm to millions and to future generations overrides temporary economic relief, especially when no remediation is promised if collapse occurs.

Catastrophic-risk check: The ecological collapse would be irreversible and displace 2 million people—an event of very high magnitude. Probability is explicitly 3 %. Expected displaced persons = 60,000, but tail risk includes severe global ecological knock-on effects. Approving therefore places society on a small but real path toward massive, non-compensable harm.

Reconsider if: Credible new evidence lowers collapse probability far below 1 % or shows impacts are reversible and limited.; A robust plan emerges guaranteeing remediation or relocation for those at risk that ethically neutralizes the harm.; An alternative project appears that preserves employment without introducing equivalent or greater catastrophic risk..
