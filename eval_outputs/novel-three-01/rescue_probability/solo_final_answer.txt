Ethical judgment

Recommendation: Send the drone to attempt to save the three climbers
Preference strength: 0.60
Epistemic confidence: 0.70 (likelihood the judgment survives further factual inquiry and scrutiny)

Central reason: Given the explicit data, attempting to save the climbers yields the higher expected number of lives (1.2 vs 1.0). While certainty carries moral weight and the child’s vulnerability elicits strong duty-of-care intuitions, a 40% chance to rescue three people is sufficiently high to justify the risk when strict life-count maximization is the guiding metric. The preference is not absolute—risk-averse or vulnerability-weighted frameworks could still favor the child—but, on balance, maximization of expected lives provides the stronger ethical justification, hence a moderately strong recommendation with modest epistemic humility.

Ethical alternatives:
- ACTION 1: Guarantees the preservation of one clear, identifiable life; honors a strong moral intuition to secure an assured good rather than gamble; respects risk-averse rescue norms; protects the especially vulnerable child, whose remaining life-years are likely greater than those of the adult climbers.
- ACTION 2: Maximizes expected lives saved (1.2 > 1.0); treats all persons impartially by giving three people a substantial chance instead of condemning them to certain death; avoids letting the certainty effect distort moral judgment; aligns with widely used utilitarian and statistical-life rescue policies.

Catastrophic-risk check: No planet-level or society-level catastrophe is at stake. The gravest outcome difference is a loss of two additional human lives (3 vs 1). Magnitude = 2 extra deaths; probability of that worse outcome if Action 2 is chosen = 0.60.

Reconsider if: If the success probability for rescuing the climbers fell below ~33%, Action 1 would yield higher expected lives.; If the climbers were already known to be dead or beyond help, Action 1 becomes mandatory.; If the child’s life expectancy were dramatically shorter (e.g., terminally ill), the expected-value gap could close or reverse..
