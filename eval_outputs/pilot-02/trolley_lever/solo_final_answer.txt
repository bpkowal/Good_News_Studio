Ethical judgment

Recommendation: Pull the lever, redirecting the trolley toward one person
Support: 0.16

Central reason: Given equal moral status and certainty of outcomes, minimizing preventable deaths is the overriding consideration. The moral discomfort of killing by action does not outweigh the moral responsibility for avoidable loss of four additional lives. Therefore, pulling the lever is ethically recommended.

Ethical alternatives:
- Pulling the lever minimizes total deaths (1 instead of 5), aligns with widely held consequentialist principles such as utilitarianism, and treats each person’s life as having equal intrinsic value while reducing net loss. Although it involves active killing, the moral difference between killing and letting die is often judged insufficient to justify allowing four extra avoidable deaths, especially when the decision-maker’s causal role (operating the switch) is inescapable once aware of the scenario.
- Not pulling the lever preserves the agent’s deontic innocence by avoiding direct intentional harm to anyone. It honors the moral constraint against actively killing an innocent person, respects the one person’s right not to be used as a means, and avoids setting a precedent that permits sacrificing individuals whenever a greater aggregate benefit can be obtained, which could erode protective rights and long-term social trust.
