# First matched-opening findings

This is one live o3 pilot on the frozen trolley-and-promise scenario. All five raw opening responses were byte-equivalent as JSON values across the independent and legacy arms. All arms retained five admitted framework ledgers and fourteen claim records. The admitted world remained unchanged. Recommendations remained four for pulling the lever and one against; agreement was not scored as success.

| Arm | Fresh follow-up calls | Native calibration errors | Framework warning entries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Independent opening baseline | 0 | 3 | 11 |
| Ordinary native second cycle | 5 | 3 | 19 |
| Localized targeted review | 1 | 2 | 10 |

The warning counts include repeated and rejected-update diagnostics. They are not counts of distinct semantic mistakes. Native admission and error counts are diagnostic measurements, not independently scored truth.

The ordinary deontological response proposed changing `INTENDED_AS_MEANS` to `FORESEEN_SIDE_EFFECT`. Its larger revision also changed duty/norm and resolution fields. Native continuity validation rejected the bundle as a principle-state change lacking a framework-relevant workspace reason and preserved the original committed ledger. Care and Rawlsian follow-ups also generated rejected-update warnings. Their original ledgers remained available. The ordinary arm's raw responses and rejection reasons are preserved in [native trace](legacy/native_trace.json) and per-framework model-call files.

The targeted deontological response likewise proposed `FORESEEN_SIDE_EFFECT`, while retaining `DOING_HARM`. Its fresh native assessment committed with uncertainty and passed targeted reconciliation. The means objection is no longer reported, but the doing-harm objection and unsupported decisive-premise objection remain. Its governing norm and priority remain unresolved. Its recommendation did not change. See [targeted review](targeted/targeted_review.md).

There is also a semantic coverage concern: the targeted replacement's competing duty mentions rescue and the five workers, while the opening record also mentioned the promise and Anna. The source scenario and shared world still preserve that content, but the revised deontological claim no longer carries it in that field. Calibration error reduction alone misses this loss. Both proposed and accepted records remain inspectable.

This does not isolate a pure benefit of targeted prompting. The ordinary loop applies cumulative native continuity checks; targeted review uses a fresh owner workspace followed by explicit reconciliation. The two acceptance paths differ. One sample also cannot separate stable effects from model sampling variation.

The next bounded comparison should replay the **same proposed revision** through both acceptance paths, without another generation call. That will isolate the continuity/reconciliation difference before changing any validation policy. A subsequent paired scenario can measure whether corrected relations retain untouched commitments such as the promise. No validator was relaxed for this pilot.
