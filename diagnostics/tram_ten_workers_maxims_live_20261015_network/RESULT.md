## What happened to the candidate maxims

The deontological reply supplied both maxims on its first call. It proposed pulling to save ten as impermissible, and refraining to avoid killing as permissible. These remain attributed interpretations, not facts about Dana’s intentions.

The new fields expose an unresolved inference: pulling is classified as a foreseen side effect, but its stated means is still “Tram strikes side-track worker.” The review describes the chosen means as redirecting the tram in its answer, yet leaves the contrary means field unchanged. Its universalization explanation continues to assume that this foreseeable death falls under the prohibition it is trying to establish. This sample makes the disputed reasoning more inspectable; it does not demonstrate a stronger justification.

The deontological review declared both assigned objections RESOLVED. Both native objections stayed OPEN, and its operative position remained ABSTAIN despite its recorded preference for not pulling. The known causal-topology checker limitation remains: the admitted graph uses ENABLES where its doing-harm check demands CAUSES/ACCELERATES. That technical objection does not settle whether redirection is morally prohibited.

The residual Rawlsian review was rejected by native review; its prior attributed argument remains visible and its two scheduled objections remain OPEN. Care retains contested claims. No shared world was changed.
