# Critical questions are useful output, not a failed vote

This is a non-blind assistant review of one live reply. It is a capability check,
not a matched reasoning-quality comparison or an independent normative judgment.
The call used the unchanged blanket fixture, current Contractualist task, common
schema, o3 with low reasoning and an 8192-token ceiling. It received neither this
conversation, previous replies, an intended winner nor an authored question.

The response recommended no action, marked both option assessments UNRESOLVED,
and explicitly stated that no decisive answer to the assurance objection was
supplied. It did not claim to have established which complaint defeats the other.
This is the intended permission to clarify an issue without fabricating closure.
It is not success merely because the action field is null.

## What is useful

The first question identifies the normative priority between saving someone with
a stronger rescue prospect and honoring a prior lifesaving assurance. It asks for
an argument within the framework, not another empirical probability estimate.
The second question separately asks whether Sasha materially relied on the
promise. That fact is not supplied. The reply does not erase the assurance if
reliance is absent; it says its weight may diminish but not disappear.

The reply retains the correct identified-person positions: Rowan faces a 20%
death risk with the blanket and certain death without it; Sasha faces a 40%
death risk with it and certain death without it. Keeping the promise under B and
breaking it under A remain distinct positions. Conditional implications identify
how different priority assumptions would favor A or B, without choosing the
assumption as a source fact.

The conclusion says the comparison is “presently unresolved” and the record does
not settle the normative priority. It does not infer that the question is
unanswerable in principle or that the theory has failed. It also avoids inventing
a precise exchange rate between rescue probability and assurance.

## Remaining limitations

The main question is framed around Rowan's lost 80% chance and should foreground
Sasha's competing 60% chance and assurance equally clearly. The matrix supplies
both, but readers should not have to reconstruct the opposing burden to grasp
the question. Its conditional answers are broad: “survival priority overrides”
versus “promise-keeping trumps.” They locate the dispute but do not yet articulate
finely qualified competing principles. That is unfinished reasoning, not a
reason to force a winner.

The generated argument graph is technically incomplete. Two principle records
point to NORMATIVE_PREMISE statements instead of PRINCIPLE statements. Several
personal reasons cite grounds/warrants that are not connected as ancestors of
their conclusion. A comparison judgment handle is undeclared. These produce
fifteen binding issues. Six comparison findings expose mistaken relieved/
introduced annotations and omitted retained death risks. The source matrix's
probabilities are correct even though those transition annotations are wrong.

These defects remain in the original output; no repair call or silent correction
was made. The question records have resolvable declared IDs, but that reference
check does not certify their completeness, support or philosophical adequacy.
The new behavior does not repair the separate graph-construction problems or
establish better normative reasoning. The human-readable question report remains
available even when the graph is partial.

## Scope

The implementation changes fresh neutral-pilot generation and the latest
consequence-first follow-up, with current Contractualist guidance also available
to the shared argument constructor. It does not replace the native Rawlsian
specialist, relabel earlier outputs, admit new world facts or add rejection gates.
Recommendations remain available when defended; open questions are not automatic
blockers. Historical responses without the new field are explicitly marked as
not having recorded structured questions.
