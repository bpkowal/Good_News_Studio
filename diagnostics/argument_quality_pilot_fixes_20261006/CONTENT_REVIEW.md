# Matched argument-quality rerun after bounded fixes

The arithmetic and promise-evidence mappings now work. Overall moral argument
quality has not yet shown a dependable improvement. The two prompt arms still
produce mostly the same preferences and largely asserted priority rules.

The rerun made 18 live o3 adapter calls. [Controls](controls.json) confirm matching
source inputs, framework commitments, model, token ceiling and one call per cell
between arms. The three worlds passed fresh admission and remained unchanged.
These are independent specialist passes, not a full RelEnt collective verdict.

## Demonstrated changes

| Check | Previous pilot | New pilot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Utilitarian cells with rejected survivor arithmetic | 6 of 6 | 0 of 6 |
| Arithmetic verification | None of the six survivor comparisons verified | All six comparisons verified |
| Stated promise available as shared source evidence | Not projected | STIPULATED in all six promise-case ledgers |
| Promise-case checklist deontology | Provisional, ledger with uncertainty | COMMITTED, no native framework warning |
| Care submissions without committed ledgers | 1 of 6 | 2 of 6 |

The arithmetic result is more than a stochastic live improvement: replaying the
old utilitarian response now verifies the same authored calculation. Count proof
is tied to an admitted named single-person identity and an explicit survival
statement. Its scope is `RESTRICTED_SURVIVOR_ENDPOINT_NOT_TOTAL_WELFARE`;
welfare magnitude and duration are still unknown.

The promise card records occurrence of the commitment, exact source offsets and
participant identities. It does not assert treatment of Cara or assign normative
priority to the promise. In standard deontology's promise run, the material claim
binds directly to `PROP:WORLD:RECOGNITION:4697fd89cd506cc4`. Native review still
questions that response's omission/perfect-duty classifications. Recognizing
the premise has not automatically justified its conclusion.

## Preferences and arguments

All authored baseline responses prefer Ben. All authored probability-swap
responses prefer Cara. In the promise case, both arms' authored preferences are
Ben for utilitarian and Care, and Cara for deontology. Standard utilitarian's
native result withholds an operative recommendation as provisional; standard
deontology retains Cara provisionally. Both Care promise submissions have no
committed ledger. Authored preference, operative recommendation and ledger
admission are distinct outputs.

The checklist deontologist uses the same perfect negative promise-duty
classification for both actions and avoids claiming omission is agent-caused
harm. That is a cleaner submission than its previous response. Its priority
argument is still essentially “perfect duties override imperfect.” Acceptance
does not supply the missing defence of that priority in this case.

Care's promise arguments now describe treatment of Ben as straining trust and
treatment of Cara as preserving trust. Both still report `SELECTED` while their
relational records say `CONTESTED`. Native validation rejects them with
`unresolved Care ranking must remain provisional`. The complete model-authored
records are retained in each framework's `argument_work_record.json`; they have
no authority to modify the shared world.

## Operational fixes verified separately

- Ranking distinguishes `SOLE_NONFALLBACK`, `FAMILY_TWICE_SUPPORT`, member-level
  separation, invariance and unresolved alternatives. Fallbacks retain their
  graphs as noncompeting members. Same-family differences remain checked.
- Duty inconsistency can consume one existing repair call. Saved-response tests
  verify two-call success and still-contradictory failure without changing duty
  types in code. One-call pilot cells intentionally do not use this allowance.
- Review retains every open conflict and permits one residual round for an
  unreviewed framework, capped at two issues. A native offline run reaches Care
  then Rawlsian, records four attempted issues and two unattempted issues, and
  keeps every failed objection open.
- Retirement requires an accepted relevant change with surviving record IDs and
  fresh validation. Status-only Care resolutions, unchanged ledgers, and deleted
  records cannot retire warnings. Isolated reasons remain attributed, not proved.

The [regression log](../argument_quality_fixes/regression.log) records 129 passing
tests. The [native integration patch](../../integrations/parliament_z10/argument_quality_fixes.patch)
was checked against its predecessor and reproduces all four changed files.
Care and Rawlsian validators and the world-state schema were not changed.

The next remaining argument task is to make the author explain the contested
priority and compile a provisional submission from that same argument record.
It should preserve the Care reasons rather than force `RESOLVED`, and test whether
the explanation actually answers the competing claim. Adding more blueprint
families would not address these remaining failures.
