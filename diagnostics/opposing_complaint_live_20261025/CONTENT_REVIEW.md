# Actual opposing complaints: better targets, unreliable answers

The follow-up reached the right opposing person in all four cases. It did **not**
establish more reliable justification. The focused replies often elaborated their
preferred conclusion while introducing unsupported facts or misdescribing the
alternative. All eight replies called the complaint `ANSWERED`; that label is not
supported as an independent resolution of these objections.

This is one non-blind assistant content review, not an independent normative
oracle. It does not decide that cooling Jo or honoring Sasha's promise must win.
The criticism is that the submitted comparisons do not adequately justify their
conclusions from the supplied facts and stated normative assumptions.

## What was tested

Twelve live calls completed on October 26: four independent complaint assignments,
then two responses per assignment. The directory retains its preparation name.
All used `o3-2025-04-16`, low reasoning; all finished with `stop`. Reply arms shared
the exact saved Contractualist argument, source, assigned complaint, framework
instruction, response schema and 8192-token ceiling. Only the final instruction
differed. All four assignments were frozen before the first reply.

The selector received neither this review, a preferred complainant, another
framework's answer, nor an intended winner. It selected Jo against Plan B in both
cooling cases and Sasha against allocation to Rowan in both blanket cases. These
are relevant opponents of the original recommendations. The focused arm was
asked to concede their complaint, defend its priority premise and compare the
countercomplaint to the feasible alternative.

The scenarios are the earlier authored pilot fixtures, not newly admitted worlds.
No source, original argument, operative ledger or Parliament acceptance rule was
changed. [Offline verification](verification.json) confirms hashes and matching;
[verify.py](verify.py) reproduces those checks without API calls. Ninety-eight
related regression tests passed during implementation. Structural success and
matching do not establish semantic support.

## Assignment quality matters

The selected **person** was appropriate; the assigned **grounds** were not reliably
faithful. Both response arms received the same fallible assignment, including its
binding findings and an instruction that it could be mistaken.

- Cooling selectors described Jo's exposure as life-threatening or imminent and
  other households' benefits as moderate discomfort. The source supplies greatest
  heat exposure, not that medical severity ranking. Those could be conditional
  arguments, but are not established facts.
- The promise selector treated reliance as established. The promise is supplied;
  psychological reliance and its consequences are not.
- The no-assurance selector claimed giving Sasha the blanket would leave Rowan
  with an “80 % risk of death.” The source instead makes the nonrecipient's death
  certain. This fabricates an asymmetry between the two excluded people.

All four assignments also failed exact source-quote binding. Several quotations
include literal outer quotation marks; the no-assurance quotation additionally
rewrites the source. These formatting findings are separate from the substantive
errors above. Original outputs remain intact; no quote was silently repaired.

### Cooling: a real complaint is now visible, but new entitlements appear

The ordinary refusal reply says, “We now have two rival severe-burden objections,
neither clearly overriding the other.” That is more responsive than answering Sal
as though Sal were disadvantaged by B. But it depends on an unverified comparison
of medical danger and livelihood risk. Its suggestion of “short-term loans or
savings” is not source evidence, even when described as typically available.

The focused refusal reply says each of the twelve households can reject “losing
their promised protection,” and concludes that their complaints “collectively
outweigh Jo's single” rejection. No promise to those households exists in the
source. A proposed benefit is not already-held protection. A contractualist
argument about numbers would also need its own defended warrant.

In the consent case, the focused reply says choosing B can mitigate harm “for all
thirteen of us together.” B expressly excludes Jo. It also moves from Jo's
“gravest and most immediate” danger to “losses that are not basic-needs deprivation”
without evidence. The ordinary arm's retreat to multiple defensible options is
not automatically correct either: it also says both are reasonably rejectable,
without explaining how that permits either option. Hedging is not a resolution.

### Assurance: do not solve the challenge by rewriting what was promised

Sasha's survival chance rises from zero to 60% if she receives the blanket. Her
loss under allocation to Rowan is therefore 60 percentage points, not 40. Both
replies get this wrong. The focused countercomplaint additionally says honoring
the promise raises Sasha's survival chance “from 60 % to 100 %.” Neither endpoint
describes the choice in the source.

The focused reply argues through a rule that could have prevented betrayal by
qualifying promises in advance. That might inform future rescue policy. It does
not establish that Ellis's existing promise contained that qualification. Nor
does calling betrayal a “non-fatal moral harm” remove Sasha's lost chance to live.
An argument may still favor Rowan, but it must compare Rowan's 80-point lost
chance with Sasha's 60-point lost chance **and** the assurance-related reason,
without asserting a universal numerical priority as an already settled fact.

### No assurance: one partial correction, followed by a familiar mistake

The ordinary arm usefully says, “both principles impose the same qualitative
burden (certainty of death) on someone.” It then claims the preferred rule gives
“any generic hiker” an 80% chance, versus 60%. Those probabilities belong to the
recipient. No probability distribution over occupying each person's position is
supplied. Even an explicitly assumed equal identity lottery would not assign
these recipient probabilities to everyone. Reintroducing an anonymous-person
calculation does not answer the identified excluded person's complaint.

The focused arm repeats the selector's false Rowan counterfactual in its
restatement, corrects it in its countercomplaint, then states, “Both principles
leave exactly one person dead.” Each guarantees at least one death; the blanket
recipient can also die. This is not merely a disputed philosophical weighting.

## What the comparison supports

Actual opponent recognition improved relative to the saved openings. Seven of
eight replies have quote-binding findings, while even the one with no binding
findings contains the generic-hiker error. Citation bookkeeping therefore cannot
stand in for reasoning review.

There is no clear consistent focused-arm gain in defending the disputed bridge
or preserving source uncertainty. Relevant reversal conditions appear in both
arms, but unexplained numerical cutoffs, compensation variants unavailable in the
current choice and requests for an additional procedure do not settle the current
objection. Such counterfactuals may be legitimate boundary tests if explicitly
labeled; they cannot become available actions or source facts.

One sample per arm/case cannot establish a treatment effect. The shared structured
schema already asks both arms for comparisons; the ordinary arm is not an
unconstrained baseline. Comparison against the saved opening also includes extra
inference budget. Changed recommendations and longer explanations are not proof
of improvement. Both ordinary cooling replies dropped the explicit action choice;
both focused replies retained B. All blanket replies retained A, provisionally.
None of those changes, by itself, determines argument quality.

## Next engineering implication

Keep the actual-opponent response mechanism, but do not promote this prompt as a
reasoning fix yet. The next bounded comparison should give the response an
explicit, person-by-option consequence record alongside the original source and
ask it to correct unsupported premises in the complaint **before** defending a
normative priority. Preserve the actual promise separately from proposed future
policies, and require any priority claim to name its normative warrant and what
remains contestable. This is a better input to deliberation, not another admission
gate or an instruction to change the moral answer. It has not been run here.
