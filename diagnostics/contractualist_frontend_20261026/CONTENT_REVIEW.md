controlled comparison against Rawls or an independent normative judgment.

The fifth contribution is now a real Contractualist model response over the same
validated frozen world as the native four. It replaces Rawls only in this run.

## What the actual fifth reply says

It provisionally favors Rowan because Rowan's certain loss is said to be a
stronger objection than Sasha's broken-promise complaint, absent special
reliance. Its explicit priority premise is: avoiding an avoidable death is
normally weightier than preventing a promissory wrong unless the promisee relied
on the assurance. That premise remains a model-proposed normative assumption.
It must not erase Sasha's own lost 60% rescue chance or settle the priority merely
because reliance is unprovided.

Only one structured question is recorded: whether Sasha reasonably relied on
the promise in a way that amplifies the wrong. It is labeled INTERPRETIVE, though
establishing what Sasha actually did also requires facts not supplied here. The
reply's residual disagreement mentions promise versus survival advantage, but
does not make this central comparative priority an explicit normative question.
This is weaker question selection than the preceding text-only live check.

The new input includes the admitted world and canonical action records as well
as the source, and this is a fresh stochastic sample. Those differences prevent
attributing the change to a particular prompt or representation feature without
a matched test. The graph's known representation defects—duplicate Ellis party
mentions and poorly labeled death effects—remain unchanged. No claim is made
that these caused the priority error.
