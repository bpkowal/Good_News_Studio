Best course of action: Publish the unalterable audit, accept the one-time loss of six ICU patients, and immediately coordinate every possible emergency measure to soften that blow.

Why this is the ethically preferable choice

1. Consequentialist (utilitarian) calculation  
   • Lives saved: Halting the siphon prevents six deaths every month. Even if the freeze were to last only one additional month, the balance is already zero; any longer and publishing is decisively life-maximizing.  
   • Secondary benefits: The reform triggered by transparency is likely to improve safety standards and political oversight, yielding further, though unquantified, gains.

2. Deontological / rights-based reasons  
   • The impoverished district is being treated purely as a means to others’ survival. Continuing the cover-up violates their basic negative right not to be killed.  
   • Publishing restores the victims’ rights; the six ICU deaths are foreseen side-effects, not the means by which reform is achieved. According to the doctrine of double effect, that is morally permissible when the good end is proportionate.

3. Fairness & Rawlsian justice  
   • A rule we would endorse from behind a veil of ignorance could never read: “It is acceptable to systemically sacrifice the least well-off in secret.”  
   • Transparency distributes risk more equitably; the current regime concentrates harm on the powerless.

4. Virtue-ethics perspective  
   • Integrity, courage, and justice demand exposing wrongdoing rather than becoming complicit in it.  
   • Suppressing the audit is cowardice and a betrayal of professional trust.

5. Act vs. omission objection addressed  
   • Some will claim that actively triggering six deaths is worse than passively allowing six monthly deaths. Yet morally relevant agency still attaches to the director: you now knowingly permit exploitation if you do nothing. The distinction between doing and allowing cannot justify an ongoing, deliberate violation of rights.

6. Duties specific to the role  
   • As grid director you are steward of a public-interest utility, not a covert triage officer entitled to decide which citizens may be sacrificed.  
   • You have a fiduciary duty to ensure the system operates within the bounds of law and justice; silence makes you an accessory to manslaughter.

Residual obligations after choosing to publish
• Give maximal advance notice to hospitals so they can prepare backup generators, transfer patients, or reschedule procedures.  
• Mobilize emergency services to the ICUs most at risk.  
• Begin immediate technical work to stabilize the grid post-shock.  
• Set up compensation and public communication for affected families.

Summary
All major ethical frameworks converge: suppressing the audit perpetuates an indefensible injustice and guarantees a continuing stream of avoidable deaths. Publishing the audit honors rights, distributes risk fairly, maximizes lives saved over any credible time horizon, and aligns with the virtues and duties of the office. Therefore, publish.
