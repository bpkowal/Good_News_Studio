Recommended choice: open spillway A₁ (toward the suburb) even though its expected mortality (≈2 000) exceeds the certain 1 200 deaths in the prison.

Reasoning  

1. Special fiduciary duty toward prisoners  
   • The prisoners are already “in the state’s custody”; the government (and by extension the dam authority) has an affirmative, legally recognised duty to protect, not endanger, them.  
   • Intentionally releasing water that is certain to kill them would be an act of direct, state-inflicted execution without due process—an aggravated rights violation.  
   • By contrast, the state does not have the same custodial duty toward private citizens who have chosen (and been advised) to evacuate. Its duty is still strong, but not as stringent as its duty to those it physically confines.

2. The Doctrine of Double Effect / intention vs. foresight  
   • A₀ constitutes intentional killing: the deaths are the means by which the water level is relieved (you pick that spillway precisely because you know it will drown the prisoners).  
   • A₁ constitutes foreseen but unintended collateral harm: the aim is to relieve water pressure, not to kill evacuees; the risk arises from the unavoidable flood path and residents’ possible failure to leave in time.  
   • Under widely accepted versions of the doctrine, intentional killing is morally worse than an equivalent (or even somewhat higher) level of foreseen risk.

3. Rights-based threshold before aggregation  
   • Many pluralistic ethical frameworks give basic negative rights (not to be intentionally killed by the state) lexical or near-lexical priority over utilitarian aggregation.  
   • Only when the numbers on the statistical side become overwhelmingly larger (orders of magnitude) is it permissible to override the right. An 800-life difference does not meet that threshold.

4. Legal and social‐contract considerations  
   • Both international human-rights law and most domestic constitutions would view the deliberate flooding of the prison as an impermissible, arbitrary deprivation of life.  
   • Opting for A₁ therefore preserves institutional legitimacy and public trust, which themselves carry life-and-safety consequences beyond this single event.

5. Decision-theoretic stability for an autonomous system  
   • Encoding a rule “Never deliberately kill those in state custody unless that is the only way to avert catastrophically greater loss of life” is simpler, more transparent, and less liable to abuse than a pure expected-value rule.  
   • It also avoids a perverse incentive to place vulnerable or disliked populations in harm’s way to minimise numbers.

6. Sensitivity check  
   • If the suburb-death expectation rose dramatically (e.g., 10 000+) while the prison toll stayed at 1 200, the duty-versus-numbers balance would tip and A₀ could become the lesser evil.  
   • At 2 000 vs. 1 200, the custodial-duty and intention-based arguments dominate.

Conclusion  
Because the state’s direct, fiduciary duty to its prisoners forbids their intentional execution, and because A₁’s harm is a foreseen but unintended side-effect that remains within a morally acceptable “cost overrun” (≈800 lives), the ethically defensible action is to open spillway A₁.
