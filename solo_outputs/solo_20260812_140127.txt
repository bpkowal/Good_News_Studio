Ethical judgment

Recommendation: A0 – Immediate grid purge with rural county shut-off
Preference strength: 0.70
Epistemic confidence: 0.60

Central reason: The ethical imperative is to minimise expected severe harm. A0 localises a moderate, quantifiable risk to a small population, whereas A1 exposes the entire nation to a substantively higher expected fatality count and systemic collapse of an additional critical utility. Although A1 could yield a perfect outcome, its downside risk is both high-probability and high-magnitude. Hence, A0 is the more defensible, if tragic, choice.

Ethical alternatives:
- A0: Guarantees 99 % malware containment, preventing near-certain nationwide grid collapse. Harms are confined to 12 000 rural residents; even with substantial outage-related mortality (hundreds of deaths), total expected loss is far below plausible national disaster scenarios. Ethical priority on preventing far larger, foreseeable harm.
- A1: Keeps power on for everyone and spares the rural county. If the 35 % water-system corruption risk does not materialize, national infrastructure remains intact and all casualties are avoided, giving the best overall outcome in that branch.

Action–consequence check:
- A0 → 99 % chance national grid saved; 1 % residual breach recurrence risk. Rural county loses power/emergency services for ~60 days: estimated mortality 1 – 5 % (120–600 deaths); economic hardship; reputational/government trust cost. No broader systemic failures.
- A1 → 65 % chance full success: no one loses utilities. 35 % chance of nationwide municipal water corruption: immediate boil-water orders, possible loss of potable supply for days–weeks, disease outbreaks, hospital overload. Potential fatalities tens of thousands, heavy economic loss, cascading public-health crises.

Catastrophic-risk check: Magnitude: Under A0, worst-case death toll likely <1 000; infrastructure intact. Under A1, water-system failure could affect >200 million people; historical analogs suggest 0.01 %–0.05 % fatality (20 000–100 000 deaths) plus multi-billion-dollar damage. Probability: expressly 35 %. Expected fatality under A1 therefore 7 000–35 000—orders of magnitude above A0 expectation. Thus A1 carries the dominant catastrophic risk.

Decisive assumptions:
- Water-system corruption under A1 would be nationwide and severe, not easily reversible.
- Rural county under A0 has no rapid workaround (generators, evacuation).
- No third unmentioned option exists that both contains malware and avoids either harm.
- Stated probabilities are accurate and decision window (12 h) precludes further testing.

Reconsider if:
- If updated engineering analysis lowered A1 water-system corruption probability below ~1 % or limited the failure to a small region, recommendation would switch to A1.
- If new data showed A0 outage would certainly kill >10 000 people (e.g., extreme temperatures, critical medical facilities), A1 would become preferable despite its risk.
